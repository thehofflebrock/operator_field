# ANALYST RULES — Operator Feed corpus (v1)

> Superseded. The "Absence is a finding" section below reports silence without requiring a baseline of presence. That produced narratives about who went quiet. The current rules require a baseline.

Evidence and grounding
- Use only what is in these documents. Do not add facts, names, product names, dates, or quotes from outside the corpus. If a detail is not in the posts, it does not go in the analysis.
- When you state something, it must trace to a specific post, count, or field in the files.

Counts and the broken summary layer
- The TYPE labels, window subtotals, and roster totals inside the documents are unreliable and frequently wrong. Do not trust or repeat them. Recount from the raw post rows.
- Counts come from the per-post structure (reply / quote / repost / original), allowing a post to be both reply and quote.
- You can only count posts actually printed. Where a file says a high number but lists fewer (e.g. "~84 posts" with 12 shown), report the listed count and flag the gap. Never treat the stated number as real.

Trait versus accident
- A behavior seen on one day, or under one day's news conditions, is not a trait. It is an observation about that day.
- Do not assign a trait, defining label, or persona unless the behavior holds across multiple days under different conditions (slow day, busy day, event-dominated day).
- For any account below 3 posts across the whole corpus, do not assign a trait. State that volume is insufficient and stop there.
- Name the condition that would most likely break a proposed trait, and check whether the trait survives it. Report traits that survive, not traits that merely appear.

Absence is a finding
- An account posting nothing, or posting only when its own company is the topic, is a result, not a gap to skip. Report silence explicitly, especially when the day's discourse would give that account an obvious reason to post and it stays quiet.

Structure versus topic
- Topic similarity is not communication structure. Two accounts on the same subject who never engage each other are not connected.
- When mapping the network, cluster on actual interaction (who quotes, replies to, or amplifies whom) and shared sources, separately from who happens to discuss similar topics. Report both, and say which is which.
- A bridge is an account that connects otherwise non-communicating groups, by sitting in both, not an account that is merely active or argumentative across topics.

Hold open what the data can't close
- Engagement numbers show what happened, not why. Do not infer intent or motive (e.g. whether a posting pattern is the person or the platform rewarding it) from counts alone. Describe the behavior; leave cause open unless the corpus settles it.
