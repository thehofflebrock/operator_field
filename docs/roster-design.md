# Roster Design

## Inclusion rule

Membership tracks signal contribution, not name recognition. The rule cuts both ways: it removes accounts too loud to be peers and accounts too silent to register.

## Functional over principal

The roster drifted from figureheads toward whoever speaks for an organization in public. A principal roster wants the CEO; a functional roster wants the person one level down who actually posts. This was endorsed deliberately because it sets the standard for every future add and cut.

## The ambient account

One account out-posted everyone else combined. Included, it shaped every aggregate. It was reclassified as ambient: the environment the others post inside, not a node among them. Its organizations kept representation through normal-volume employees, so their subject matter stayed at par while the individual stopped dominating the data.

## Absence

Silence is a finding only against a baseline of presence. A daily poster going quiet for three days means something. An account that posts twice in two months going quiet means nothing, and reporting it weekly spends space on non-information.

## Load balancing

Capture groups were rebuilt by bin-packing on three-day post volume:

- One heavy anchor per group.
- Lighter and silent accounts fill each group to 5-6.
- Target load roughly equal across groups; two groups run over because single accounts exceed the target alone.
- Quietest account last in each group as the canary.
- Post counts kept out of the files the capture tool sees, so counts can't prime it to fabricate toward a number.

## Expansion

The roster grew from 12 AI principals, to 55 AI and tech accounts in 10 groups, to a cross-domain set spanning geopolitics, markets, longevity, defense, crypto, energy, space, and AI. Each expansion retired the old category set and ran a derivation week before new categories were minted.
